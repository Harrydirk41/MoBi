3. Model & model choice

Distribution / permeability: Cellular partition coefficient method - Rodgers and Rowland, Cellular permeability - PK-Sim Standard
Processes: CYP3A4

A whole-body PBPK structure with Rodgers-and-Rowland tissue partitioning is appropriate for alfentanil: it is a basic, moderately lipophilic small molecule (MW 416.5, pKa~6.5) bound mainly to alpha-1-acid glycoprotein rather than albumin, and Rodgers-Rowland explicitly accounts for ionized-base partitioning into acidic phospholipids as well as neutral-lipid/plasma-protein partitioning, which is mechanistically the right choice for this compound class. Cellular permeability was calculated with the PK-Sim standard (charge/MW/lipophilicity-based) method rather than measured Caco-2/PAMPA input, which is a reasonable default for a passively permeating, non-P-gp substrate, but it means the permeability term is a structural surrogate rather than an independently anchored value. Hepatic CYP3A4-mediated intrinsic clearance was implemented as the sole elimination pathway, consistent with the known biology (extensive CYP3A4 metabolism, <1% renal excretion of parent), so GFR fraction was fixed to zero rather than estimated. Oral dosing was handled with a transcellular intestinal permeability term to capture absorption rate, since alfentanil is highly soluble (992 mg/L) and absorption is expected to be permeability- rather than dissolution-limited; no active transport process was added given the absence of P-gp substrate evidence.

4. Model equations (ODE system)

PK-Sim's standard human whole-body structure: ~15 perfused organs (lung, brain, heart, kidney, gut, spleen, pancreas, liver, stomach, muscle, skin, bone, adipose, gonads) plus arterial and venous blood pools. Each organ is further divided into plasma, blood-cell, interstitial and intracellular sub-compartments, so the solved system is one mass-balance ODE per sub-compartment (order ~10^2 states). The physiological volumes V and flows Q are fixed by the individual; only the drug terms below are model choices.

Perfused tissue (distribution)
V_t · dC_t/dt = Q_t · ( C_art − C_t / (K_p,t / R_bp) )
Each non-eliminating organ t (brain, heart, muscle, skin, adipose, bone, ...): rate in from arterial blood at flow Q_t, rate out with the venous effluent in equilibrium with tissue via the tissue:plasma partition coefficient K_p,t (R_bp = blood:plasma ratio).
Venous blood pool
V_ven · dC_ven/dt = Σ_t Q_t · C_t / (K_p,t / R_bp) − Q_co · C_ven + R_iv(t)
Collects the effluent of every organ; Q_co = cardiac output. IV doses enter here as an input rate R_iv(t) (bolus or infusion).
Arterial blood pool
V_art · dC_art/dt = Q_co · ( C_lung − C_art )
Blood is oxygenated/mixed through the lung and distributed to the organs.
Permeability-limited exchange (cellular)
V_cell · dC_cell/dt = P · SA · ( f_u·C_int − f_u,cell·C_cell / K_p,t )
Where cellular permeability limits uptake, the intracellular space is a separate state exchanging with interstitium across the membrane (permeability P × surface area SA), driven by the unbound gradient.
Liver (hepatic metabolism)
V_liv · dC_liv/dt = Q_liv·C_art + Q_po·C_po − Q_hv·C_liv/(K_p,liv/R_bp) − CL_int · f_u · C_liv
The liver receives arterial + portal (Q_po, first-pass) inflow; CYP3A4 metabolism removes drug as a first-order intrinsic-clearance sink CL_int·f_u·C_liv on the unbound concentration.
Kidney (renal filtration)
V_kid · dC_kid/dt = Q_kid·(C_art − C_kid/(K_p,kid/R_bp)) − GFR · f_u · C_kid
Glomerular filtration removes unbound drug at rate GFR·f_u·C_kid. In THIS model this term is ZERO (GFR fraction = 0): renal excretion of unchanged drug is negligible, so the renal sink is switched off.
Oral absorption (PO doses)
dA_lum/dt = −P_int · SA_int · C_lum,u ;   J_abs = P_int · SA_int · C_lum,u  →  gut wall → portal vein → liver
Dissolved drug in the intestinal lumen permeates the mucosa (specific intestinal permeability P_int × surface area), enters the gut wall, and drains via the portal vein to the liver (first-pass).

Tissue partitioning. K_p,t is not a fitted number but is computed per organ from tissue composition by the Rodgers and Rowland method — a function of effective lipophilicity, the unbound fraction f_u, drug ionization (pKa) and each tissue's lipid / water / phospholipid / protein content.

Where the fitted / fixed parameters enter:

  • Lipophilicity — sets every tissue partition coefficient K_p,t (the distribution terms in all organ ODEs).
  • Permeability — the cellular permeability P in the permeability-limited exchange term.
  • Intrinsic clearance — the hepatic elimination sink CL_int in the liver ODE.
  • Specific intestinal permeability (transcellular) — P_int in the oral-absorption flux J_abs.
  • Fraction unbound (plasma, reference value) (fixed) — f_u — scales every unbound-driven term (K_p,t, CL_int, GFR).
  • GFR fraction (fixed) — the renal filtration sink GFR in the kidney ODE.

These are the governing equations; PK-Sim generates the fully instantiated system (every organ and sub-compartment, and the exact Rodgers and Rowland K_p formula) at build time and integrates it numerically. The concentrations plotted are the peripheral-venous-plasma state.

6. Pharmacological rationale

Intrinsic clearance (0.417 L/min, sensitivity 1.0) is the dominant estimated parameter and is essentially fully determined by the IV concentration-time data (no independent literature CLint value was provided for comparison); given its sensitivity of 1.0, the fitted value can be trusted as a data-driven estimate of net CYP3A4 hepatic clearance, but it is a lumped/apparent term that will also absorb any misspecification in distribution or absorption if those are wrong. Lipophilicity was fitted to 1.49, sensitivity 0.681 (fairly well-informed by the data), but this is a clear departure from the literature logD(7.4) of 2.1-2.2 (Baneyx 2014/Jansson 2008). Because sensitivity is moderately high, this is not a case that can be dismissed as "within a wide plausible range" - the optimizer is using an effective lipophilicity appreciably lower than the measured value, most plausibly to compensate for the Rodgers-Rowland-predicted tissue distribution/Vss or for correlation with the fixed fraction unbound (0.1, itself within the reported 8.6-12% range and consistent with literature), and this trade-off should be flagged as a structural/parameter-correlation artifact rather than evidence that the compound's true lipophilicity is lower than measured. Cellular permeability (3.75e-4 cm/min, sensitivity 0.025) and specific intestinal transcellular permeability (3.84e-5 cm/min, sensitivity 0.026) are both very weakly identified by this dataset - the fits are essentially insensitive to their values, so these numbers should be treated as largely unconstrained/default-driven outputs of the PK-Sim standard method rather than confirmed physiological estimates; the visibly worse oral performance (78.2% within 2-fold vs 91.4% IV) is consistent with these low-sensitivity absorption parameters not being meaningfully calibrated by the available oral data. The fixed inputs (fraction unbound 0.1, solubility 992 mg/L at pH 6.5, reference pH 6.5, molecular weight 416.52) all match their literature anchors well and were reasonably left unadjusted.

9. Conclusion

Optimization completed and parameters were fitted, yielding an overall fit rated "acceptable" (GMFE 1.524, 86.6% of observations within 2-fold, bias 1.214 indicating a modest systematic overprediction tendency). Performance is route-dependent: IV predictions are reasonably good (GMFE 1.467, 91.4% within 2-fold) while oral predictions are distinctly weaker (GMFE 1.628, 78.2% within 2-fold, larger bias), pointing to unresolved uncertainty in the absorption description. The high-sensitivity intrinsic clearance estimate is the most credible fitted parameter, well determined by the IV data, whereas the permeability terms (cellular and intestinal) are poorly constrained (sensitivity ~0.025-0.026) and should not be over-interpreted despite their biologically plausible magnitudes. The fitted lipophilicity, despite reasonably high sensitivity, disagrees with the measured logD and should be treated as an effective, correlated parameter rather than a validated physicochemical estimate; this departure, combined with the oral shortfall, suggests the distribution/absorption compensation in the model is not fully resolved. Overall the model is usable for IV alfentanil exposure but should be considered provisional for oral predictions and refined lipophilicity/absorption characterization before extrapolation to new dosing scenarios or populations.
